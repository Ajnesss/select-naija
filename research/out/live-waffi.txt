                                    <div class="story">The Federal Government has reviewed 932 federal enactments and 731 subsidiary legislations, identifying 269 laws as obsolete, spent or repealed and recommending their exclusion from the proposed 2025 Laws of the Federation of Nigeria.

The Attorney General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, Prince Lateef Fagbemi (SAN), disclosed this on Thursday in Abuja during the presentation of the final report of the Committee on the Review, Update and Consolidation of the Laws of the Federation.

Fagbemi explained that 663 of the 932 enactments reviewed, alongside their relevant subsidiary legislation, had been recommended for inclusion in the revised 2025 edition, while the remaining 269 were found to be obsolete, spent or repealed.

He described the outcome as significant progress towards establishing an updated and consolidated body of federal laws in the country.

The minister noted that Nigeria's last official consolidation of the Laws of the Federation was conducted in 2004, adding that more than two decades of legislative, constitutional, institutional and technological developments had made another comprehensive review necessary.

According to him, several new laws and amendments have been introduced over the years, making it imperative to ensure that the country's legal framework remains current, consistent, accessible and responsive to national needs.

Fagbemi disclosed that he inaugurated the committee on October 24, 2024, with the responsibility of reviewing federal enactments and subsidiary legislation, identifying obsolete laws, resolving inconsistencies and overlaps, and producing an authoritative and accessible compilation of the country's laws.

He commended the committee, chaired by Olawale Fapohunda (SAN), for its efforts, noting that the assignment involved extensive legal research, legislative analysis, technical coordination and editorial work.

He said the committee adopted a systematic approach through 14 thematic working groups, supported by retreats and technical engagements, to harmonise its findings and ensure quality assurance.

The AGF also recognised the contributions of development partners and collaborating institutions, including the ROLAC Programme, the Law Commission of England and Wales, LexisNexis South Africa, the Lagos State Law Reform Commission and Law Pavilion.

Fagbemi said the final report would be submitted to President Bola Tinubu for onward transmission to the National Assembly for legislative consideration and necessary action.

He emphasised that successful completion of the exercise would require sustained cooperation between the Executive and Legislature.

The minister further cautioned against allowing another prolonged interval before the next consolidation, stressing the need to establish a sustainable system for monitoring new enactments, amendments and subsidiary legislation to keep Nigeria's laws updated and responsive to emerging developments.

                                    <div class="story">The Revenue Mobilisation Allocation and Fiscal Commission (RMAFC) has called for the fair and equitable distribution of the 13 per cent derivation fund among oil and gas-producing states.

The Chairman of the commission, Mohammed Shehu, made the call on Thursday in Owerri, Imo State, during the flag-off of an oil and gas wells verification exercise.

Shehu explained that the 1999 Constitution, as amended, empowered the commission to monitor revenue inflows into and disbursements from the Federation Account, including the 13 per cent derivation fund.

He stated that the commission was constitutionally mandated under Paragraph 32(a) of Part I of the Third Schedule of the Constitution to oversee revenue accruals and ensure that the derivation fund was fairly shared among eligible states.

According to him, the exercise would focus on examining and verifying the coordinates of disputed oil and gas wells, alongside newly drilled wells between 2017 and the present.

He explained that the Inter-Agency Technical Committee established for the assignment would undertake physical inspections of the affected wells, confirm the accuracy of their geographical coordinates and rely exclusively on officially approved maps.

The committee, he added, would also take relevant court judgments into consideration when addressing disputes over oil and gas wells, while forwarding its findings solely to the commission for ratification and adoption.

The RMAFC chairman described the verification exercise as critical to ensuring transparency and accuracy in revenue distribution from the Federation Account, stressing that the precise locations and coordinates of oil and gas wells determine the financial entitlements of the respective states.

                                    <div class="story">Nigeria’s crude oil and condensate production averaged 1.68 million barrels per day (mbpd) in October 2026, despite the country’s continued struggle to meet the crude oil supply requirements of the Dangote Refinery.

The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (NUPRC) disclosed this in a statement issued by its spokesperson, Eniola Akinkuotu, noting that the country had met its Organisation of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) production quota for the sixth consecutive month.

According to the commission, Nigeria’s daily output peaked at 1.79 million barrels per day during the month, while the lowest production level was recorded at 1.60 million barrels per day.

However, crude oil and condensate production declined by 0.03 million barrels per day month-on-month, representing a drop of 30,000 barrels per day or approximately 1.75 per cent.

Production figures released by the NUPRC showed that Nigeria recorded 1.70 mbpd in May, 1.73 mbpd in June, 1.677 mbpd in July, 1.677 mbpd in August, 1.71 mbpd in September and 1.68 mbpd in October.

When condensate production is excluded, Nigeria’s average crude oil output stood at 1.51 million barrels per day in October, representing 101 per cent of the 1.5 million barrels per day quota allocated to the country by OPEC.

Despite meeting its OPEC target, Nigeria has continued to struggle to supply sufficient crude oil to the Dangote Refinery, which has an installed processing capacity of 700,000 barrels per day.

About a month ago, the Minister of Finance acknowledged that the country lacked adequate crude oil production to meet the refinery’s daily feedstock requirements.

Available figures indicate that the refinery imports between 20 and 22 per cent of its crude oil requirements, while locally sourced supplies account for approximately 78 to 80 per cent.

The refinery obtains its domestic crude oil supplies through the Federal Government, mainly via the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPCL).
